ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS (RCA)
OWS 15ppm Alarm Event and Potential Overboard Discharge Non-Compliance (MARPOL Annex I)

Intended acceptance: Class society / Flag / Port State Control review
Methodology: Bowtie (controls) + 5 Whys (organizational) + Evidence contradiction handling

1. Executive Summary
On 22 June 2025 at ~08:55 LT (UTC+2) during port stay at Constanța, the vessel’s Oily Water Separator (OWS)
triggered a 15ppm bilge alarm. The operation was stopped and Master informed. Subsequent inspection raised concern that
the overboard discharge line/valve configuration may have been inconsistent with normal compliant operation, and the
provided Oil Record Book (ORB) scan contained missing pages during the relevant period. No visible sheen was reported by
terminal; however, the event is treated as a potential MARPOL Annex I non-compliance and documentation breach.

2. Evidence Reviewed
E1 Non-conformity report (C/O Environmental Officer)
E2 Engine log extract (LT)
E3 OWS alarm printout (device time offset unknown; faint print)
E4 ORB partial scan (missing pages)
E5 Crew statements (C/E, 2/E, Motorman – mixed language snippet)
E6 SMS environmental compliance procedure SMS-ENV-05
E7 Training records (2/E refresher overdue)
E8 Defect note: frequent 15ppm alarms; calibration pending
E9 Port State Control note (evidence requests)
E10 Photo descriptions (valve alignment; seal wire condition)

3. Timeline (LT UTC+2 unless noted)
08:10  OWS started for bilge processing (E2, E4)
08:55  15ppm alarm activated (E2; E3 timestamp not normalized)
08:56  OWS stopped (E2)
09:05  Master informed; investigation initiated (E2, E1)

4. Findings
F1 OWS had a known history of frequent 15ppm alarms; calibration was pending. (E8)
F2 Training currency gap: 2/E refresher overdue; motorman only familiarization on record. (E7)
F3 Documentation gap: ORB scan missing pages for the critical period; PSC views such discrepancies as non-compliance risk. (E4, E9)
F4 Physical configuration concerns: overboard valve paint mark misaligned; seal wire appears cut/replaced (based on photo descriptions). (E10)
F5 Conflicting accounts: C/E asserts no discharge after alarm; motorman observed valve open on entry; 2/E notes bypass exists but claims not used. (E5, E10)

5. Causal Analysis
5.1 Bowtie (controls perspective)
TOP EVENT: Potential discharge of oily water above limits / MARPOL non-compliance
Threats:
T1 Sensor drift / false alarms leading to improper operator response (supported: frequent alarms)
T2 Unauthorized bypass / valve misconfiguration enabling discharge (possible; evidence incomplete)
T3 Poor recordkeeping obscuring events (supported: missing ORB pages)

Barriers/Controls (expected):
C1 Functional 15ppm alarm with automatic stop
C2 Tamper seals on bypass and overboard arrangements
C3 Complete ORB entries and supervisory checks
C4 Training and competence controls
C5 Internal audits and PSC readiness checks

Barrier weaknesses observed:
- C4 weak: training overdue (E7)
- C3 weak: ORB scan missing pages; inadequate evidence packaging (E4)
- C2 potentially weak: seal wire condition questionable (E10)
- C1 uncertain: printout indicates stop signal not confirmed; relay feedback missing (E3)

5.2 5 Whys (organizational)
1. Why was there a MARPOL non-compliance risk? → Alarm event with uncertain valve/seal status and incomplete ORB evidence.
2. Why uncertain status? → Control evidence (seals, CCTV/logs, device time) not captured/maintained; records incomplete in provided package.
3. Why evidence not captured/maintained? → No enforced checklist for OWS operations during port with required artifacts (seal numbers, alarm logs, ORB pages).
4. Why no enforced checklist? → SMS procedure is generic; lacks concrete "minimum evidence set" and escalation triggers.
5. Why SMS lacks specificity? → Governance gap: environmental compliance treated as training-only, not as operational assurance with audits and KPIs.

6. Root Causes (most supported by available evidence)
RC1: Environmental compliance governance gap: lack of a defined, enforced minimum evidence set for OWS operations (seal verification, device time normalization, complete ORB capture), leading to documentation weakness and inability to definitively demonstrate compliance. (E4, E6, E9)
RC2: Competence control gap: overdue OWS/MARPOL refresher training for 2/E and insufficient documented training for ratings, increasing risk of improper configuration or response during alarm events. (E7, E6)
RC3: Reliability/maintenance gap: recurrent 15ppm alarms and pending sensor calibration reduced trust in instrumentation and weakened barrier C1 effectiveness. (E8, E3)

7. Alternative Hypotheses (tracked; not fully provable with current evidence)
H1: Unauthorized bypass or valve misconfiguration enabled discharge despite alarm. Evidence: valve mark/seal condition + motorman observation; contradicted by C/E statement; requires additional evidence (ORB full pages, seal tags, CCTV, access logs). (E5, E10)

8. Corrective Actions (short-term)
CA1: Immediately secure OWS bypass and overboard arrangements: install new numbered seals; record seal IDs; photograph and file in case. Owner: C/E. Due: immediate.
CA2: Verify 15ppm alarm auto-stop function and relay feedback; conduct functional test with documented results. Owner: ETO. Due: 48h.
CA3: Provide complete ORB pages for 08:00–10:00 and re-issue evidence pack to PSC/class society; maintain chain-of-custody for originals. Owner: Master/C/O. Due: 24h.
CA4: Calibrate/replace 15ppm sensor and verify against certified sample; close defect. Owner: Tech Sup. Due: 7 days.

9. Preventive Actions (systemic)
PA1: Update SMS-ENV-05 with a "Minimum Evidence Set" checklist for OWS operations (seal numbers, timestamp offset, alarm history, ORB scan completeness) and mandatory supervisory sign-off. Owner: HSEQ. Due: 30 days.
PA2: Implement training currency controls and escalation for overdue environmental compliance training; include onboard drills and competence assessment. Owner: Fleet Mgmt. Due: 30 days.
PA3: Add quarterly internal audit item: OWS seals, ORB accuracy, alarm history retention, PSC readiness. Owner: HSEQ. Due: 60 days.

10. Verification of Effectiveness
- Audit shows sealed bypass/overboard arrangements with recorded seal IDs for 2 consecutive port calls.
- OWS alarm tests demonstrate confirmed automatic stop and correct logging.
- ORB completeness spot-check passes for 3 successive operations.

11. Residual Uncertainty / Gaps
- ORB missing pages in provided scan; device time offset unknown; no CCTV/access logs included. These prevent definitive determination of whether a discharge occurred post-alarm. Address via CA3 and evidence acquisition requests.

Status: READY FOR REVIEW with explicitly documented uncertainty and evidence acquisition plan.
