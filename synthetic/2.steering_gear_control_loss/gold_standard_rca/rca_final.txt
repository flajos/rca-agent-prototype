ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS (RCA)
Steering Gear Loss of Control – Near Grounding

Intended acceptance: Class society / Flag review (DNV, ClassNK, LR, ABS style expectations)
Methodology: Hybrid Fault Tree (technical) + 5 Whys (organizational)

1. Executive Summary
On 08 March 2025 at ~18:16 ship’s time (UTC+1), MV SYNTHESIS experienced intermittent steering response and
erratic rudder angle indication during coastal transit. The vessel deviated from the planned track and approached
shoal water (CPA ~0.28 NM). Steering response recovered after changeover from Steering Pump A to Pump B and speed reduction.
No injuries or damage occurred. The event is classified as a Near Miss with high potential severity.

4. Findings
F1 Steering response degradation coincided with brief hydraulic pressure instability. (E5)
F2 Suction strainer differential pressure was reported high; PMS strainer cleaning overdue. (E5, E6)
F3 Rudder angle transmitter had an open defect for intermittent signal prior to the event (DEF-2271). (E6)
F4 VDR record has a gap during the critical minute; full reconstruction relies on logs + ECDIS. (E4, E2, E3)
F5 Contradiction: Bridge recalls no alarms; engine room observed local alarm. (E2, E5, E7) – treated as non-fatal inconsistency.

5. Causal Analysis
5.1 Technical Fault Tree (simplified)
TOP EVENT: Loss of effective steering / near grounding
A) Hydraulic system pressure instability leading to reduced actuator response
   A1) Pump suction restriction due to clogged/contaminated strainer (supported)
   A2) Pump mechanical fault (not supported in evidence)
B) Rudder angle feedback uncertainty leading to delayed/incorrect helm response
   B1) Intermittent rudder angle transmitter signal (supported by open defect)
C) Operational context increased consequence
   C1) Coastal passage with limited margins; reliance on autopilot (supported)

5.2 5 Whys (organizational)
1. Why did steering become unreliable? → Pressure instability + unreliable rudder feedback.
2. Why pressure instability? → Suction restriction and overdue maintenance.
3. Why overdue maintenance? → PMS interval not enforced; task backlog not escalated.
4. Why backlog not escalated? → No risk-based prioritization for safety-critical components.
5. Why no prioritization? → SMS lacks explicit criticality tagging and escalation triggers for steering gear tasks.

6. Root Causes (validated)
RC1: Inadequate maintenance control of steering gear suction strainer (overdue cleaning; marginal oil quality), leading to intermittent hydraulic pressure instability. (E5, E6)
RC2: Unresolved intermittent rudder angle transmitter defect (DEF-2271), contributing to uncertain feedback during the event. (E6, E2)
RC3: SMS/PMS governance gap: absence of criticality-based escalation for safety-critical navigation equipment maintenance tasks. (E6, E8)

7. Contributing Factors
CF1: Partial VDR data gap reduced immediate post-incident reconstruction quality (process risk). (E4)
CF2: OCR checklist quality prevents reliable confirmation of pre-departure steering test (documentation quality). (E9)
CF3: Coastal navigation environment increased potential severity; reliance on autopilot at 12.5 kn reduced reaction time. (E2, E3)

8. Corrective Actions (short-term)
CA1: Clean/replace steering gear suction strainers; inspect hydraulic lines; restore normal DP readings. Owner: C/E. Due: immediate.
CA2: Perform hydraulic oil sampling and replace/filtration as per maker guidance; trend particulate counts. Owner: Tech Sup. Due: 7 days.
CA3: Rectify DEF-2271: test/replace rudder angle transmitter, verify calibration with independent reference. Owner: ETO. Due: 7 days.

9. Preventive Actions (systemic)
PA1: Update PMS: add safety-critical tagging for steering gear tasks; enforce escalation if overdue > X days. Owner: Fleet Mgmt. Due: 30 days.
PA2: Update SMS: define minimum evidence for coastal passage readiness (verified steering tests; record retention). Owner: HSEQ. Due: 45 days.
PA3: Introduce periodic navigation equipment health review including defect closure KPIs. Owner: HSEQ. Due: 60 days.

10. Verification of Effectiveness
- Post-maintenance steering tests in confined waters with recorded pressure trends and rudder feedback stability.
- PMS audit to confirm no overdue safety-critical steering tasks for 2 consecutive cycles.

11. Residual Uncertainty / Gaps
